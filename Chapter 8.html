Chapter 8: Information Security and Cyber Law

Digital Society and Computer Ethics

A digital society is one where advanced technologies, especially Information and Communication Technologies (ICTs), are deeply integrated into daily life and culture, facilitating communication, learning, and commerce. This integration brings numerous benefits like social connectivity, communication speed, versatile working, and learning opportunities. However, it also introduces ethical dilemmas that require careful consideration, which is where computer ethics comes in.
    
Computer Ethics refers to a set of moral principles and guidelines that govern the responsible use of computers and information technology. These ethics help individuals, organizations, and governments make intelligent decisions about actions involving computers, ensuring they are morally right and do not harm others or society.

Ethical Issues in Computing

Privacy:
   Respecting the privacy of others means not accessing or sharing personal information without consent.

Intellectual Property:
   Respecting copyrights, licenses, and patents of software and digital content. Software piracy is a major ethical concern.

Accuracy:
   Ensuring the accuracy of information processed and stored by computers.

Access Rights:
   Preventing unauthorized access to computer systems and networks.

Harmful Actions:
   Avoiding the use of computers to hurt others or spread misinformation.

Digital Divide:
   The gap between those who have access to information technology and those who do not.

Concept of Information Security

Information security, often abbreviated as "Infosec," is the practice of protecting information and information systems from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction. Its primary goal is to ensure the Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA) of data.
    
Confidentiality:
   Ensuring that information is accessible only to authorized users.

Integrity:
   Maintaining the accuracy and completeness of information.

Availability:
   Ensuring that authorized users have timely and reliable access to information.

Threats to Information Security

A security threat is a potential danger that could exploit vulnerabilities in a computer system and cause harm. Threats can be intentional or accidental.
    
Interception:
   Unauthorized access to or stealing of information.

Interruption:
   Making services or data unavailable.

Modification:
   Unauthorized alteration of data.

Fabrication:
   Introducing false data into a system.

Malicious Software (Malware):
   Programs designed to harm computer systems.

Hacking:
   Gaining unauthorized access.

Phishing:
   Deceptive attempts to obtain sensitive information.

Denial of Service (DoS) Attacks:
   Overwhelming a system with traffic.

Encryption and Decryption

Cryptography is the art and science of protecting information and communications through the use of codes, ensuring that only authorized people can read and process the information.
    
Encryption:
   Converts readable plaintext into unreadable ciphertext.

Decryption:
   Converts ciphertext back into readable plaintext.

Types of Encryption

Symmetric Key Cryptography (Private Key Cryptography)

Uses a single secret key for both encryption and decryption.

Both sender and receiver must possess the same key.

Faster encryption and decryption process.

Reliable for fewer users or communications.

Asymmetric Key Cryptography (Public Key Cryptography)

Uses two different keys:

   Public Key for encryption.

   Private Key for decryption.

The public key is shared openly.

The private key remains secret.

Supports digital signatures and authentication systems.

Concept of Cybercrime

Cybercrime refers to any criminal activity that involves the use of computers or the internet, either as a tool to commit the crime or as the target of the crime.

Types of Cybercrimes

Hacking:
   Unauthorized access to computer systems.

Phishing:
   Fake emails or messages to steal sensitive information.

Identity Theft:
   Stealing another person's personal information.

Malware Attacks:
   Viruses, worms, Trojans, ransomware, and spyware.

Cyberbullying/Cyberstalking:
   Online harassment and intimidation.

Software Piracy:
   Illegal copying or distribution of software.

Online Fraud:
   Internet-based financial deception.

Ransomware:
   Malware that encrypts data and demands payment.

Implications of Cybercrime

Financial Loss

Data Breach and Loss

Loss of Privacy

Reputational Damage

Malicious Software and Spam

Malicious Software (Malware)

Malware is a general term for software specifically designed to disrupt computer operations, steal information, or gain unauthorized access.

Types of Malware

Viruses:
   Attach themselves to legitimate software.

Worms:
   Spread across networks without a host program.

Trojan Horses:
   Disguised as legitimate software.

Spyware:
   Secretly monitors user activity.

Rootkits:
   Enable unauthorized administrator-level control.

Ransomware:
   Encrypts files and demands payment.

Phishing

Phishing is a deceptive technique used to acquire sensitive information by pretending to be a trustworthy organization through emails or messages.

Spam

Spam refers to unwanted and unsolicited messages sent in bulk, often for advertising or malicious purposes.

Preventive Measures against Malware and Spam

Avoid Pirated Software

Keep Software Updated

Protection from Cybercrime

Effective protection from cybercrime requires a combination of technology, security policies, and user awareness.

Cybersecurity Measures

Access Management

Security Monitoring

Data Encryption

User Education

Firewall

A firewall acts as a digital barrier between a computer network and external networks. It monitors incoming and outgoing traffic based on predefined security rules and blocks unauthorized access.

Antivirus Software

Antivirus software detects, prevents, and removes malicious software from computer systems using signature-based detection and behavioral analysis.

Data Backup

Data backup is the process of creating secure copies of important data and storing them in separate locations to recover information after hardware failure, accidental deletion, or cyberattacks.

Intellectual Property Right (IPR)

Intellectual Property refers to creations of the mind such as inventions, literary works, artistic works, designs, symbols, names, and images used in commerce.

Types of Intellectual Property Rights

Copyright:
   Protects literary, artistic, musical works, software, books, music, and websites.

Patents:
   Protect new, useful, and non-obvious inventions.

Trademarks:
   Protect brand names, logos, and slogans.

Importance of IPR in Digital Content

Protects Creators

Prevents Piracy

Fosters Innovation

Builds Trust

Concept of Cyber Law in Nepal

Cyber Law is the legal framework that governs activities in cyberspace, including computers, networks, data, software, and the internet.
    
In Nepal, the primary cyber law is the Electronic Transaction Act (ETA), 2063 BS (2008 AD), which legalizes electronic transactions and provides legal recognition to digital signatures.

Sample Board Exam Questions

1. Define Cybercrime. Explain Any Three Types of Cybercrimes with Their Implications. (8 Marks)

Solution

Cybercrime is any criminal activity involving computers or the internet as a tool or target of crime.

Three Types of Cybercrimes

Phishing:
   Tricks users into revealing sensitive information.

Implication:
   Financial loss and identity theft.

Hacking:
   Unauthorized access to computer systems.

Implication:
   Privacy breaches and data theft.

Malware Attacks (Ransomware):
   Encrypts victim's files and demands payment.

Implication:
   Data loss and financial extortion.

2. What is Information Security? Explain the Roles of Firewall, Antivirus Software, and Data Backup. (8 Marks)

Solution

Information security is the practice of protecting information and information systems from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, or destruction while ensuring confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

Roles in Protection

Firewall:
   Blocks unauthorized access and harmful traffic.

Antivirus Software:
   Detects, prevents, and removes malware.

Data Backup:
   Creates secure copies of important data for recovery after data loss.

Chapter 1. Compter System

Introduction of Computer

A computer is an electronic device that accepts raw data and instructions from users, processes it according to a given set of instructions, stores the processed data, and produces meaningful information as output. The word "Computer" is derived from the Latin word "Computare," which means 'to calculate'. Modern computers are versatile machines capable of performing complex mathematical calculations accurately and quickly, as well as handling tasks like creating documents, videos, games, and presentations.

Key Characteristics of a Computer:

1)Speed: Computers can perform calculations and process information at incredibly high speeds, often measured in millions of instructions per second (MIPS). 2)Accuracy: Computers are highly accurate and perform tasks without errors, provided the input data and instructions are correct. 3)Diligence: Unlike humans, computers do not suffer from boredom, fatigue, or lack of concentration. They can perform repetitive tasks with the same speed and accuracy. 4)Versatility: A computer can perform a wide range of tasks, from complex scientific calculations to simple word processing, making it a truly multipurpose device. 5)Storage Capacity: Computers have the ability to store vast amounts of data and information, both temporarily (primary memory) and permanently (secondary memory). 6)Reliability: Computers are generally reliable and consistent in their performance.

Evolution of Computer Technology (Generations)

The evolution of computers is categorized into generations, each marked by significant technological advancements that led to smaller, faster, cheaper, and more efficient machines.

1. First Generation (1940s-1950s): Vacuum Tubes

-Technology: Used vacuum tubes for circuitry and magnetic drums for memory.
-Characteristics: Very large in size, consumed enormous amounts of electricity, generated a lot of heat, and were expensive. Programming was done in machine language.
-Examples: ENIAC, UNIVAC.

2. Second Generation (1950s-1960s): Transistors

-Technology: Replaced vacuum tubes with transistors.
-Characteristics: Smaller, faster, more reliable, and consumed less power than first-generation computers. High-level programming languages like FORTRAN and COBOL were introduced.
-Examples: IBM 7000 series, CDC 1604.

3. Third Generation (1960s-1970s): Integrated Circuits (ICs)

-Technology: Used Integrated Circuits (ICs), which combined multiple transistors on a single silicon chip.
-Characteristics: Further reduction in size and cost, increased speed and efficiency. Operating systems were introduced, allowing computers to run multiple applications simultaneously.
-Examples: IBM System/360, PDP-8.

4. Fourth Generation (1970s-Present): Microprocessors

-Technology: Utilized Very Large Scale Integration (VLSI) technology, leading to the development of microprocessors. A microprocessor contains all the components of a computer's central processing unit (CPU) on a single chip.
-Characteristics: Personal computers (PCs) became widely available. Graphical User Interfaces (GUIs), mouse, and handheld devices emerged. Networking and the internet began to develop.
-Examples: Apple Macintosh, IBM PC.

5. Fifth Generation (Present and Beyond): Artificial Intelligence (AI)

-Technology: Based on Artificial Intelligence (AI) and parallel processing.
-Characteristics: Aim to develop computers that can understand natural language, learn, reason, and make decisions. Focus on areas like robotics, expert systems, and natural language processing.
-Examples: AI-powered systems, quantum computing, neural networks.

Measurement Unit of Processing Speed and Storage Unit

Understanding how computer speed and storage are measured is essential for evaluating performance.

1.Processing Speed:

The processing speed of a computer, particularly its CPU, is measured in cycles per second, known as Hertz (Hz).

Hertz (Hz): One cycle per second.
Kilohertz (KHz): 1,000 Hz
Megahertz (MHz): 1,000 KHz or 1,000,000 Hz
Gigahertz (GHz): 1,000 MHz or 1,000,000,000 Hz (Most modern CPUs operate in the GHz range).
A higher clock speed generally indicates faster processing, though other factors like core count and architecture also play a significant role.

2.Storage Unit:

Computer storage is measured in bits and bytes, which represent binary digits (0s and 1s).

Bit (b): The smallest unit of data, a binary digit (0 or 1).
Nibble: 4 bits.
Byte (B): 8 bits. This is the fundamental unit for storing a single character (e.g., 'A', '1', '$').
Kilobyte (KB): 1,024 bytes.
Megabyte (MB): 1,024 KB.
Gigabyte (GB): 1,024 MB.
Terabyte (TB): 1,024 GB.
Petabyte (PB): 1,024 TB.
These units help quantify the capacity of memory and storage devices like RAM, hard drives, and pen drives.

Types of Computers (Super, Mainframe, Mini, Microcomputers)

Computers can be classified based on their size, speed, cost, and capabilities.

1. Supercomputers:

Characteristics: The fastest, most powerful, and most expensive computers. They can perform trillions of calculations per second.
Applications: Used for highly complex scientific and engineering problems, weather forecasting, climate research, nuclear research, and advanced simulations.
Example: Summit (IBM), Fugaku (Fujitsu).

2. Mainframe Computers:

Characteristics: Large, powerful, and expensive computers capable of supporting hundreds or thousands of users simultaneously. They have high processing power and large storage capacity.
Applications: Used by large organizations for critical applications like bulk data processing, enterprise resource planning (ERP), transaction processing (e.g., banking, airlines), and scientific research.
Example: IBM zSeries.

3. Minicomputers (Midrange Computers):

Characteristics: Smaller and less powerful than mainframes but more powerful than microcomputers. They can support dozens to hundreds of users.
Applications: Used by small to medium-sized organizations for specific tasks like process control, data management, and scientific instrumentation.
Example: DEC PDP series, IBM AS/400.

4. Microcomputers (Personal Computers - PCs):

Characteristics: Smallest, least expensive, and most common type of computer. Designed for individual users.
Applications: Widely used in homes, schools, offices, and small businesses for tasks like word processing, internet browsing, gaming, and multimedia.
Types: Desktops, Laptops, Tablets, Smartphones, Workstations.
Example: Desktop PCs, Apple MacBooks, smartphones. In Nepal, microcomputers are ubiquitous in educational institutions, government offices, and personal use.

Mobile Computing and its Applications

Mobile computing refers to the use of portable computing devices and wireless networks to access information and applications from virtually anywhere. It has revolutionized how we interact with technology and the world.

Key Concepts:

1.Portability: Devices are small and lightweight, allowing users to carry them easily.
2.Connectivity: Relies on wireless networks (Wi-Fi, cellular data like 4G/5G) for internet access.
3.Interactivity: Often features touchscreens, voice commands, and sensors for user interaction.

Applications of Mobile Computing:

1.Communication: Instant messaging, video calls, email, social media (e.g., Viber, WhatsApp, Facebook widely used in Nepal).
2.Education: E-learning platforms, educational apps, online research, accessing digital notes like these.
3.Business: Mobile banking (e.g., eSewa, Khalti in Nepal), online shopping, mobile payments, remote work, sales force automation.
4.Navigation: GPS services, mapping applications (e.g., Google Maps).
5.Entertainment: Mobile gaming, streaming music and videos, e-books.
6.Healthcare: Fitness trackers, health monitoring apps, telemedicine.
7.Emergency Services: Quick access to emergency contacts, location sharing.
8.Concept of Computer Architecture and Organization
9.Computer Architecture: This refers to the conceptual design and fundamental operational structure of a computer system. It describes what the computer does and what it needs to do. It includes the instruction set architecture, memory organization, and input/output mechanisms. It's like the blueprint of a building.
10.Computer Organization: This refers to the physical implementation of the computer architecture. It describes how the computer system is structured and how its components are interconnected to implement the architecture. It deals with the operational units and their interconnections that realize the architectural specifications. It's like the actual construction of the building based on the blueprint.

In essence, architecture focuses on the user's perspective (what is visible to the programmer), while organization focuses on the hardware's perspective (how the components are connected and function).

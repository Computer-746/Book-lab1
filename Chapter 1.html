Chapter 1: Computer System

Introduction of Computer

A computer is an electronic device that accepts raw data and instructions from users, processes it according to a given set of instructions, stores the processed data, and produces meaningful information as output.

The word "Computer" is derived from the Latin word "Computare," which means "to calculate." Modern computers are versatile machines capable of performing complex mathematical calculations accurately and quickly, as well as handling tasks like creating documents, videos, games, and presentations.

Key Characteristics of a Computer

1. Speed
   Computers can perform calculations and process information at incredibly
   high speeds, often measured in millions of instructions per second (MIPS).

2. Accuracy
   Computers are highly accurate and perform tasks without errors,
   provided the input data and instructions are correct.

3. Diligence
   Computers do not suffer from boredom, fatigue, or lack of concentration.
   They can perform repetitive tasks with the same speed and accuracy.

4. Versatility
   A computer can perform a wide range of tasks, from scientific calculations
   to simple word processing.

5. Storage Capacity
   Computers can store vast amounts of data in primary and secondary memory.

6. Reliability
   Computers are generally reliable and consistent in their performance.

Evolution of Computer Technology (Generations)

The evolution of computers is divided into generations, each marked by major technological advancements that made computers smaller, faster, cheaper, and more efficient. 

1. First Generation (1940s–1950s): Vacuum Tubes

   Technology:
      Used vacuum tubes for circuitry and magnetic drums for memory.

   Characteristics:
      - Very large in size.
      - Consumed enormous electricity.
      - Generated excessive heat.
      - Programming was done in machine language.

   Examples:
      ENIAC, UNIVAC.


2. Second Generation (1950s–1960s): Transistors

   Technology:
      Replaced vacuum tubes with transistors.

   Characteristics:
      - Smaller and faster.
      - More reliable.
      - Consumed less power.
      - High-level languages like FORTRAN and COBOL were introduced.

   Examples:
      IBM 7000 Series, CDC 1604.


3. Third Generation (1960s–1970s): Integrated Circuits

   Technology:
      Used Integrated Circuits (ICs).

   Characteristics:
      - Reduced size and cost.
      - Increased speed.
      - Operating systems allowed multiple applications.

   Examples:
      IBM System/360, PDP-8.


4. Fourth Generation (1970s–Present): Microprocessors

   Technology:
      Used VLSI technology and microprocessors.

   Characteristics:
      - Personal computers became common.
      - GUI and mouse were introduced.
      - Networking and the Internet developed.

   Examples:
      Apple Macintosh, IBM PC.


5. Fifth Generation (Present and Beyond): Artificial Intelligence

   Technology:
      Based on AI and parallel processing.

   Characteristics:
      - Understands natural language.
      - Learns and makes decisions.
      - Used in robotics and expert systems.

   Examples:
      AI-powered systems, Quantum Computing, Neural Networks.

Measurement Unit of Processing Speed and Storage Unit

Understanding processing speed and storage units helps evaluate computer performance.

1. Processing Speed

The processing speed of a CPU is measured in Hertz (Hz), which represents cycles per second. 

Hertz (Hz)       = 1 cycle per second
Kilohertz (KHz) = 1,000 Hz
Megahertz (MHz) = 1,000 KHz
Gigahertz (GHz) = 1,000 MHz

Most modern CPUs operate in the GHz range.

2. Storage Unit

Computer storage is measured in bits and bytes. 

Bit (b)       = Smallest unit of data
Nibble        = 4 bits
Byte (B)      = 8 bits
Kilobyte (KB) = 1,024 Bytes
Megabyte (MB) = 1,024 KB
Gigabyte (GB) = 1,024 MB
Terabyte (TB) = 1,024 GB
Petabyte (PB) = 1,024 TB

Types of Computers

Computers are classified according to their size, speed, cost, and capability.

1. Supercomputers

Characteristics:
   - Fastest and most powerful computers.
   - Perform trillions of calculations per second.

Applications:
   Weather forecasting, climate research,
   nuclear research, and scientific simulations.

Examples:
   Summit (IBM), Fugaku (Fujitsu)

2. Mainframe Computers

Characteristics:
   - Large and powerful.
   - Support thousands of users simultaneously.

Applications:
   Banking, airlines, ERP systems,
   transaction processing, and research.

Example:
   IBM zSeries

3. Minicomputers

Characteristics:
   - More powerful than PCs.
   - Support dozens to hundreds of users.

Applications:
   Process control, data management,
   and scientific instrumentation.

Examples:
   DEC PDP Series, IBM AS/400

4. Microcomputers (Personal Computers)

Characteristics:
   - Smallest and most common computers.
   - Designed for individual users.

Applications:
   Word processing, internet browsing,
   gaming, and multimedia.

Types:
   Desktop, Laptop, Tablet,
   Smartphone, Workstation

Examples:
   Desktop PCs, Apple MacBooks, Smartphones

Mobile Computing and Its Applications

Mobile computing refers to using portable devices and wireless networks to access information and applications from almost anywhere.

Key Concepts

1. Portability
   Devices are lightweight and easy to carry.

2. Connectivity
   Uses Wi-Fi and cellular networks (4G/5G).

3. Interactivity
   Supports touchscreens, voice commands,
   and built-in sensors.

Applications of Mobile Computing

1. Communication
   WhatsApp, Viber, Facebook, Email

2. Education
   E-learning, online research, digital notes

3. Business
   eSewa, Khalti, online shopping,
   remote work

4. Navigation
   GPS and Google Maps

5. Entertainment
   Mobile gaming, music, videos, e-books

6. Healthcare
   Fitness tracking, telemedicine

7. Emergency Services
   Location sharing and emergency contacts

Concept of Computer Architecture and Organization

Computer Architecture

Computer Architecture refers to the conceptual design and operational structure of a computer system. It includes the instruction set, memory organization, and input/output mechanisms. It can be compared to the blueprint of a building.

Computer Organization

Computer Organization refers to the physical implementation of the computer architecture. It explains how hardware components are connected and work together. It can be compared to the actual construction of a building based on its blueprint.

In summary, architecture focuses on what is visible to the programmer, while organization focuses on how the hardware components are connected and function.
